The Story

Born from the forests
of Dang

In the eastern reaches of Gujarat, where the Sahyadri range meets dense teak forest, the district of Dang has been home to woodcraft for centuries.

VANYRA is not a marketplace. It is a deliberate effort to find the finest objects made by the hands of Dang artisans — people who have shaped wood and bamboo their entire lives — and offer them to homes that understand the value of what is real.

Every piece carries the fingerprints of its maker. The slight unevenness in a bowl's rim. The way the grain runs across a tray's face. These are not flaws — they are proof of something that no machine can reproduce.

The Maker

Shankar Bhai Vasava

Master Woodworker · Dang District, Gujarat

Shankar has been shaping teak and bamboo since he was twelve years old, learning at his father's workshop in a village without a proper road. He does not use a lathe. He does not sand with machines. Every piece he makes takes at least two days — and it shows.

He describes his process simply: "The wood already knows what it wants to be. I just help it get there."

The Craft

Made by hand.
No shortcuts.

I

Material Selection

Wood and bamboo are chosen for grain, weight, and character. We reject pieces that don't meet a simple standard: would we keep this ourselves?

II

Hand Shaping

The form follows the material. Artisans work with the grain, never against it — coaxing shape rather than forcing it. This takes time. That is the point.

III

Natural Finishing

A single coat of cold-pressed linseed oil. Nothing synthetic. Nothing that masks the wood — only what deepens and preserves what is already there.
